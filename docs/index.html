NRS³ on T³ — every torus has its star

On the torus T³ = dx × dy × dz every axis is closed: the last position is linked to the first. Each axis carries the closed transport T° (ring adjacency / 2) and the position P. At the maximal current state c their fluctuations open the angle θ, and cos²θ is the light share, 1 − cos²θ the dark share. With 3 positions per axis, the canonical T³ of Lean, every axis resolves 4/5 and keeps 1/5 dark, θ = arccos(2/√5) = 26.57°, and Robertson 1934 reads det Σ = (5/4)³ |det Ω| = 125/64 |det Ω|. The open cube with 3 positions keeps nothing dark: closing the axis is what leaves the dark share. It runs on its own: the transport bar fills once, then a new random torus, 3 to 25 positions per axis, with transport moving Ψ and the view turning. Drag either panel to turn both.

The eight corners: position, momentum and their sum

follows the torus, Ψ(t) and the turn above · drag any panel to turn all

Everything here is the torus above, at the same time: the same sizes, the same Ψ(t) of the transport, the same turn. The corners {0, d − 1}³ of the box above are neighbours through the three seams: on the torus they are one 2 × 2 × 2 cube. The colour is the staggered sign ε = (−1)x+y+z, Dirac's β: it anticommutes with the transport, so (T° + mε)² = T°² + m². In momentum the transport with the Kogut–Susskind signs vanishes at the 8 corners (±π/2)³ of the Brillouin cube: the doublers. A closed axis has a closed momentum circle, so every right mover comes with a left mover; the indices ±1 of the 8 corners add to 0 (Nielsen–Ninomiya, the Poincaré–Hopf count on T³). Below, their sum: each doubler as the phase θ·x on the corner cube. With the closed fibre w (3 positions) as a fourth axis, D₄ = D₃ + ε Tw, D₄² = Tx² + Ty² + Tz² + Tw²: the fibre is a Kaluza–Klein mass, 1, −½, −½, and the light level ½ comes twice, once per sense of turn.

The angle at c on every ring

At the maximal current state every closed axis opens an angle θ, never zero: the dark share is positive. With 3 positions it is exactly arccos(2/√5) = 26.57° (light 4/5, Lean); with 4 or more positions it is computed here, between about 45° and 50°: the browser finds c as the top eigenvector of K = i[T°, P] (Jacobi diagonalization, double precision) and reads the angle from it. ○ θ at c · ● θ at Ψ(t). The page runs on its own: the transport bar fills once, then a new random torus, and the bar fills again. Press Tour to stop on a torus, Transport to pause.

The octahedron of δ

drag to turn · dashed: the largest δ

Each closed axis with d positions carries δ(d) = 1/cos θ − 1 at c. Put a vertex at ±δ on every axis: the octahedron of the torus. The canonical 3 × 3 × 3 gives the smallest, δ(3) = √5/2 − 1 ≈ 0.11803 on every axis (exact). Its volume is (4/3)·𝒱, with 𝒱 = δ(dx) δ(dy) δ(dz); the values for 4 or more positions are computed here.

Tension and the angle

|t(t)| against θ(t), one panel per axis

The tension of an axis is t = ⟨K⟩, K = i[T°, P]. In the band |t| > 1 every state keeps a positive dark share (TorusDark.darkShare_pos_of_band) and det|NRS³| is strict on T³ for every state with the three axes in the band (TorusStrict.robertson_det_torus_lt). The maximal tension is the state c: √6/2 with 3 positions (ClosedThree.tension_maxState); for every d it lies in [1, 1 + 2/(d − 1)] and tends to 1 as the ring grows (ClosedAxis.one_le_cMax, ClosedAxis.cMax_le, ClosedAxis.tendsto_cMax): the current concentrates on the seam. Press Transport: Ψ(t) starts at c and leaves it.

The windings: redshift from the seam

▶ Wind or move n

On T³ the light that leaves a galaxy comes back to it: the same galaxy is seen again after every winding, at the apparent distance D = d₀ + nL (TorusCover.infinite_paths). The windings alone keep the colour: the spectrum of a state never changes (EventClock.weight_evolve). Every winding crosses the seam of the axis, the link that closes it and carries one quantum. The one input: at each seam the light keeps the light share of the ring of 3 at c, 4/5, and leaves 1/5 to the dark share. Then 1 + z = (5/4)ⁿ = exp(κ (D − d₀)) with κ = log(5/4)/L, Hubble's law z ≥ κ (D − d₀), and the light runs (5/4)ⁿ times slower: no event in it goes from 0 to 1 before (5/4)ⁿ π/(2ΔE). With κ = H₀/c the size of the axis is L = log(5/4) c/H₀.
